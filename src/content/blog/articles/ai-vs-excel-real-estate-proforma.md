**Direct answer:** Excel offers total control and transparency but is slow, fragile, and jurisdiction-blind. AI-assisted modelling offers speed, instant scenario recalculation and built-in market benchmarks — but demands verification. The professional workflow today is hybrid: an AI engine drafts and stress-tests the proforma in hours; Excel or an auditable export handles final tuning and committee review.

This is not a religion war. It's a question about which tool owns which stage of the work.

## 1. What Excel is genuinely good at
Transparency. Every formula is inspectable, every assumption visible. It is the universal language of finance — any analyst on earth can open your model and follow the logic. For bespoke structures, forensic work, and one-off analyses, it remains unbeatable. Nobody is taking Excel away.

## 2. Where Excel breaks
Time: a full development proforma built properly takes days to weeks. Fragility: one broken link or circular reference can silently corrupt outputs. Version chaos: "final_v7_REAL.xlsx" is a real risk-management problem. And payment-structure blindness: a US-style template mis-models Dubai's escrow milestone releases and Australia's 10/90 progress payments — wrong cashflows from row one, with no error message to warn you.

## 3. What "AI-assisted" actually means (it's not a chatbot guessing numbers)
A serious AI modelling engine is a structured system: payment-structure logic — native presets for regimes like Dubai's Law No. 8, Malaysia's HDA and Australia's 10/90, plus configurable rules (no escrow, custom splits, staged retentions) for any other market — automated construction S-curves and equity waterfalls, AI-generated market research for benchmarks, and one-click scenario shocks. The mathematics remains deterministic — the AI assembles and computes; it does not improvise your IRR. What changes is iteration speed: change an assumption and the entire model, narrative and charts recalculate in seconds.

## 4. The trust question committees will ask
Three checks separate a professional tool from a black box: **Can you see the assumptions? Can you export the model and audit it? Do you own the output?** If a platform hides its workings or locks your file, walk away. Equally important: know where your project data lives — confidentiality is a fiduciary issue, not an IT footnote.

## 5. The hybrid workflow top shops actually run
AI engine for the first draft and the scenario exploration — dozens of downside shocks in the time Excel takes one. Then export to Excel (or a fully auditable report) for final tuning, sensitivity tables and the committee pack. Judgement stays human throughout: which assumptions are defensible, which risks matter, whether the deal deserves capital.

## 6. When Excel alone is still the right call
Highly bespoke joint-venture waterfalls with negotiated promotes. Litigation and forensic assignments. Firm-standard models where the template itself is the deliverable. And any situation where the structure is so unusual that no engine's assumptions framework fits. Know your exceptions — don't automate the exceptional.

## Where most people get it wrong
Trusting AI output without inspecting assumptions — garbage in, garbage out, just faster. Clinging to Excel out of habit while a faster bidder tables the numbers first: in acquisitions, speed is strategy. Buying an "AI wrapper" that is really a chatbot bolted onto a spreadsheet with no payment-structure logic. And ignoring data privacy until a client asks where their numbers went.

## The workflow today
The question is no longer "AI or Excel?" It is "which stage does each own?" Draft and stress-test at machine speed; refine and defend at human speed; keep an auditable trail from first assumption to final page.

**Key takeaways:** Excel = control and audit · AI = speed and scenario breadth · The hybrid is the professional standard · Demand visible assumptions, exportable models, data ownership · Payment-structure logic is the real differentiator · In acquisitions, the finished model wins the deal.

*Excel answers the question you ask it. A good engine answers the three you didn't have time to.*
